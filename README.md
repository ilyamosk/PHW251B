## Week 3

### Lab Module 1 — Summary Statistics (03_summary-stats.Rmd)

Module 1 covers summary statistics, which are an important part of data wrangling and the exploratory data analysis (EDA) process for deriving meaningful measures from your data. This is where we find insight into our data by examining continuous data for its center and spread and categorical/nominal data for its frequencies and occurrence. This lab module will go over common tools to investigate and derive summary statistics from datasets with EDA and data visualization in mind.

### Lab Module 2 — Bar Charts (03_bar-charts.Rmd)

Module 2 covers bar charts. In this module, we would like you to explore a dataset and create a bar chart of your choice (i.e., basic/regular, dodged, or stacked) and follow the principles of good graphical design. We recommend you use this as an opportunity to step out of your comfort zone and try creating a bar chart you haven't yet explored.

## Week 9

### Lab Module 1 — Building a Baseline Table 1 (09_table-1.Rmd)

Module 1 covers how to build a baseline Table 1. A Table 1 (also called a baseline characteristics table) summarizes the key demographic and clinical characteristics of the study participants before the main analysis begins. It helps readers understand who was included in the study and determine whether comparison groups are similar at baseline or differ in important ways that could influence the results. Because it provides essential context for interpreting the findings, Table 1 is a standard feature of most clinical and epidemiologic research studies.

## Week 10

### Lab Module 1 — Plotly and Interactive tables (10_interactive-part-1.Rmd & 10_interactive-part-2.Rmd)

Module 1 covers various interactive plots and tables that can be created in R with different packages. We will be extending various types of plots that we have been working with thus far in ways that allow for increased engagement and flexibility for our audiences. Interactive plots are incredibly powerful in R, since the reproducibility can allow for on-the-fly dashboards and interactive reports that need minimal upkeep after the initial coding. Creating interactive plots also encompasses the ideals of accessibility needed for static visualizations, and requires additional care when considering the user interactivity and experience. We will try to keep this in mind while exploring options in packages such as `plotly`, `datatables` and others.

R files and datasets will be available on DataHub.
